Browser Media Converter (FFmpeg.wasm)

Convert audio/video locally in your browser (no backend). If something fails, the Worker logs will show why.

How to use
  1. Click Load Engine (first load may take time).
  2. Drop a file (audio or video).
  3. Choose output format + quality controls.
  4. Click Convert and download the result.

Some formats/codecs are experimental in FFmpeg.wasm builds. If one fails, try MP4/WebM for video, WAV/MP3/AAC/FLAC for audio.

            Advanced controls (what they do)

            

              Leave a field blank to keep source / default. Some encoders might not exist in wasm builds (then ffmpeg will error in the log).
            


            
Audio

            

              

                
Bitrate (kbps)

                
                
Controls compression quality for MP3/AAC/Opus/Vorbis. Higher = bigger file.

              

              

                
Sample rate (Hz)

                
                
Resamples audio. Common: 44100 (music), 48000 (video).

              

              

                
Channels

                
                
Downmix or force stereo. Mono reduces size.

              

            


            
Video

            

              

                
FPS

                
                
Frame rate. Higher = smoother but heavier.

              

              

                
Scale (w:-1)

                
                
Resize video. Use -1 to keep aspect ratio.

              

              

                
Codec

                
                
Chooses video encoder. Availability depends on the wasm build.

              

              

                
CRF

                
                
Quality knob (mainly H.264/VP9). Lower = better quality, bigger file. Typical: 18–28.

              

            


            
GIF

            

              

                
GIF FPS

                
                
Controls animation smoothness vs file size.

              

              

                
GIF scale (w:-1)

                
                
Downscale GIFs to avoid huge outputs.
